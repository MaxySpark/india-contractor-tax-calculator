Assumptions, limits & official sources

What this comparison assumes

  • You are an Indian resident individual. Contractor income qualifies for professional presumptive taxation, with 50% declared profit by default.
  • The presumptive receipt limit is ₹50 lakh, or ₹75 lakh when cash receipts do not exceed 5%. Receipts above the applicable limit need a different tax calculation.
  • The US case meets export-of-services conditions and uses a valid LUT. The Indian cases are domestic services, with GST either added to or included in the quote.
  • There is no other income, income taxed at special rates, foreign tax credit, GST input credit, or additional tax deduction. Fees exclude separately accounted GST.
  • Actual contractor costs reduce cash only. They are not deducted again from presumptive income. Employee payroll deductions also reduce cash only.
  • Salary means gross cash salary, not CTC. The salary case includes the applicable standard deduction; employee services are outside GST.
  • Each case represents a full 12-month alternative. This is not a calculation for a year split between export, Indian contracting and employment.
  • Rules are fixed to FY 2026–27 and do not update automatically. Figures are estimates; statutory rounding and filing details can change the final return.
